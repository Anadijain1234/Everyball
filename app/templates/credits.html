Where the numbers come from

Everyball is built on the work of people who spent years writing cricket down. Thank you.

Charles Davis, The Test Match Database

Ball-by-ball records and scorecards for Test cricket from 1877, rebuilt by Charles Davis from scorebooks, newspapers and broadcasts (sportstats.com.au). Used with his kind permission.

Cricsheet

Ball-by-ball data for Tests and ODIs from the 2000s to today, by Stephen Rushe and contributors (cricsheet.org), under the Open Data Commons Attribution License.

ESPNcricinfo

Scorecards, ball-by-ball commentary and batter-against-bowler tables from ESPNcricinfo's archive (espncricinfo.com), used with permission. ESPNcricinfo's year-by-year match lists are also how we check that no match is missing.

ODI scorecards dataset on Kaggle

Scorecards for every ODI from 1971 to 2024, with players' dates of birth and styles, compiled by bhuvaneshprasad on Kaggle.

Bill Frindall, The Wisden Book of Test Cricket

Figures from the book are used to cross-check Test scorecards. Only numbers are used; none of the book's text.

test-cricket-tours.co.uk

Facts about tours and series, used for checking.

How the numbers are kept honest. Where two sources cover the same match, they are compared batter by batter. Every innings' balls must add up to its scorecard. Every year's match count is checked against ESPNcricinfo's own list. Ball-by-ball figures always say how many matches they come from.
